# Case study — one language made many providers comparable

*A public catalog of micro-credentials (mikrokvalifikatsioon.ee).*

## The situation

Dozens of providers — universities, business schools, training companies — offer
short, recognised micro-credentials. Each describes them its own way: different
words for outcomes, different units for volume, different (or no) skill labels. A
learner cannot compare two offers side by side. An employer cannot tell what a
credential from one provider really means next to another's. The market is real
but illegible.

## What was done

Before any of it could be published, the catalog had to be **gathered and cleaned**
— pulled from many providers and public sources, reconciled, kept current. That
groundwork is most of the real work, and it never stops: providers change their
pages, data drifts, the soil must be tended every season.

On that clean ground, each programme is published in one shared shape (Credential
Commons): the same outcome structure, the same volume units side by side, the same
skill alignment, each with a stable address you can look up. No provider changes its
internals — only the public description is made to speak one language.

## What it unlocked

- **Comparability.** Two credentials from two providers now sit on the same terms —
  a learner and an employer can actually compare them.
- **A quality mirror.** Running the catalog through the CC validator immediately
  reported where data was incomplete — programmes missing their credit value or
  their learning outcomes. The gaps were surfaced honestly, not hidden.
- **Open data.** As one shape, the catalog became queryable: *which credentials
  teach skill X, at what level, across every provider?* Research and market
  analysis fall out for free.
- **No authority claimed.** The catalog certifies nothing. It carries each
  provider's own credential, made readable. That is the whole point — CC is
  infrastructure the credentials travel on, never a competing stamp.

## What each reader gets

- **Agents & developers:** point the validator at the feed → an instant conformance
  and gap report; consume one JSON-LD shape instead of many provider formats.
- **Business:** a legible market — learners choose better, providers see exactly
  where their descriptions are incomplete, everyone speaks one language.

*Edge this proves: CC is for comparability and open data across many providers, not
only transplant between two — and it is infrastructure, never an authority.*
